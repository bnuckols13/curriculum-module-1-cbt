Adults · 45 min · 4–5 people · CBT Module 1

CBT Overview

Ten minutes of teaching inside a conversation. The group gives its own first thoughts about one situation, the screen shows how those thoughts lead to different feelings, body reactions, and behavior, and the discussion moves from that example to an ordinary moment from their week. Run it with the screen shared.

Small, everyday moments. The thought, not the whole story behind it. No numbers, no bodies, no foods.

Part 1 · Intro, check-in, getting ready

:00–:10

Arrive, then one shared situation everyone answers at once.

Check-in · :00–:05

Name, and one word for how you're arriving.

What's your energy like today: low, medium, or high?

Getting ready · :05–:10

It's 2 a.m. You're in bed and you hear a loud crash in the kitchen. What's the first thing that goes through your mind?

Everyone types an answer in the chat and hits enter at the same time. Read a few out loud. Then: "Everyone heard the same crash and had a different first thought. Let's look at what happens next."

Part 2 · Inquiry and engagement

:10–:35

About thirteen minutes of psychoeducation on the screen, then twelve of discussion.

Teach · same situation, different first thought · :10–:14

Click through the three readings of the crash, then "Our answer" with one the group gave.

Point: the crash didn't change. The first thought did, and the feeling, body, and behavior followed it.

Teach · the five-part model · :14–:19

Click each part. Read the definition, then the crash example underneath.

Point: thoughts, feelings, body, and behavior all affect each other inside a situation. Name the automatic thought: fast, on its own, feels like a fact, often a guess.

Teach · how a pattern keeps itself going · :19–:23

Step through the loop one click at a time.

Point: avoiding brings relief now, and the thought comes back stronger because it never got tested. Ask, "Does this loop look familiar to anyone, from anywhere in life?"

Discuss · :23–:35

With the crash, which part showed up first for you: a thought, a feeling, or your body?

Think of an ordinary moment this week when your mood shifted, even a little. What was going on, and what went through your mind?

Type it on the board as they talk, one column at a time. Read the row back to them before moving on.

Looking at the board, what do you notice?

If the first thought had been different, what else might have changed?

Say more.Anyone else?What happened right before that?What did you notice in your body?What did you do next?What else could it have meant?

Part 3 · Next steps and takeaways

:35–:45

Go around, then the practice for the week, then read back the board.

  • What's one idea from today you want to remember?
  • Which part do you usually notice first in yourself?
  • For this week: catch one moment and write down the situation, the thought, the feeling, and what you did. The full thought record is there for anyone who wants more columns.
  • One word for what you're leaving with.

Notes

  • Keep the teaching short. Click, say the point once, move on.
  • In the discussion, ask before you explain. Write what they say on the board in their words, and read it back.
  • Let silence sit about seven seconds.
  • Keep moments small. If someone starts into a big one, thank them and ask them to bring it to their therapist this week.
  • If "I feel fat" comes up as a feeling, ask once what feeling might be underneath it, and move on.
  • If someone hears this as "think positive," say the goal is noticing the first thought and asking what else it could mean.
  • If someone says something like "everyone would be better off without me," check in with them directly right away under the program's risk protocol and route to their therapist the same day.
